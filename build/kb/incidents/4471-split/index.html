The three problems

Parser reads month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first, and the shared parser reads every date month first, so 7 April goes out as 4 July. It shows up on every channel and every tenant. Fix: parse with the tenant’s locale, which causes.ts already knows how to ask the platform for.

The booking-v3 hint tells people to type month first. Under its date field, booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY. People do what the hint says, and the form reads the field the tenant’s way, so the same swap appears for a different reason. Only the three tenants on booking-v3 see it, and only through the form. Fixing the parser will not fix this; the hint or the form must change.

A one-day move. A handful of reports describe no swap at all: the month is right and the day is off by one, or two clients in different time zones got different days for the same meeting. That is a time-zone boundary, not a date format. The desk read these correctly and noted them in desk-notes.json, then filed them under the parser by tenant. The table above counts them the same way, inside the parser row, and gives them a ticket of their own so they stop waiting for a fix that cannot touch them.

Why one ticket held all three

Decision 0001 merges on the subject line alone. The twelve subject lines in this ticket are shared across all three problems, so subject matching could not tell them apart. The intake tag could not help either: it is a guess from the reporter’s browser locale, and it disagrees with the tenant directory on a meaningful share of reports, in both directions. The one place that knew the difference was the report body, which the rule says nobody has to read.

Outcome

The desk closed #4471 on 5 October. The parser is fixed (fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales), so ticket #1 is closed as completed. The booking-v3 form was retired rather than fixed, so ticket #2 is closed as not planned. Neither change touches a one-day move across a time-zone boundary, so ticket #3 stays open.

Lessons

  • A status of known describes the words on the page, not the data under them. Read the rendered tables before trusting a Callout.
  • A footnote with a count in it is a ticket nobody filed yet.
  • Merging on subject is fast, and it hides bugs that share a symptom. When a merged ticket passes a few hundred reports, sample the bodies before calling it one fix.
